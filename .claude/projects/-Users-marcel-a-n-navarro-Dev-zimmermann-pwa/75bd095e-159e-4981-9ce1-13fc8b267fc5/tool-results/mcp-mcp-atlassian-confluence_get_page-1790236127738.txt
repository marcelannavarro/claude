Problem Statement\\n--------------------\\n\\n### 1.1 Context\\n\\nThe Zimmermann storefront serves a VIC (Very Important Customer) segment that has access to exclusive products not available to general customers. SFCC has no native mechanism to restrict product visibility at the customer group level — all products are visible to all users regardless of segment. There are 6–8 VIC Exclusive styles per season on average, and they routinely graduate to general availability, so the flag must be reversible.\\n\\n### 1.2 Problem\\n\\nSite Merchandisers have no way to flag a product as VIC Exclusive in Business Manager. Without this, VIC Exclusive products are either surfaced to all customers in browse and search (undermining the exclusivity proposition) or must be withheld from the catalogue entirely (a merchandising limitation). There is no per-product visibility gate tied to customer group membership.\\n\\n### 1.3 Scope Framing — Suppression, Not Access Control\\n\\nThis section is the load-bearing framing for the whole document. In v0.3 it also carries the justification for accepting a client-side enforcement point, so it is worth reading before §3.\\n\\nAC5 and AC6 require the product's PDP *and* the VIC Exclusive PLP to be fully accessible — and the product purchasable — by **any** customer holding the URL, regardless of customer group or login state. SFCC-303 is therefore a **merchandising suppression** story: hide VIC Exclusive products from general browse, search and crawlers, while leaving them open to anyone who has the link.\\n\\nIt follows that suppression does **not** need to be tamper-resistant. A customer who strips a refinement parameter gains access to something they were already entitled to view and buy. There is no confidentiality boundary to defend here. Genuine gating is deliberately deferred: purchase restriction is [SFCC-628](https://zimmermannworkboard.atlassian.net/browse/SFCC-628) and private-marketing-URL access is [SFCC-629](https://zimmermannworkboard.atlassian.net/browse/SFCC-629), both currently To Do.\\n\\nTwo consequences follow, and both are load-bearing later in this document:\\n\\n* A client-side enforcement point is acceptable (§4.2). This is what makes the v0.3 recommendation viable at all — under the v0.1 gated reading it would have been unacceptable.\\n* On any error or indeterminate state, the correct behaviour is to **fail open** (§5.4). Wrongly hiding general catalogue from a real shopper is a worse outcome than showing an openly-purchasable VIC product.\\n\\nv0.1 of this document was written against the opposite (gated) interpretation and specified a PDP controller guard returning HTTP 404 to non-VIC customers. That guard is withdrawn — under the current ACs it would be a defect, not a feature. See §8 for the decision trail.\\n\\n### 1.4 Success Criteria\\n\\n| **AC** | **Criterion** |\\n| --- | --- |\\n| AC1 | A Site Merchandiser can set `isVICExclusive` on a product in Business Manager and have it take effect on the next search index, with no developer involvement and no republishing or recreation of the product. |\\n| AC2 | A product flagged `isVICExclusive = true` is not displayed in any category or PLP listing to a customer outside the VIC customer group. |\\n| AC3 | The same product does not appear in site search results for a non-VIC customer, and does not contribute to any refinement counts. |\\n| AC4 | A logged-in VIC customer sees the product in listings and search results as normal, with the VIC Exclusive badge per [SFCC-225](https://zimmermannworkboard.atlassian.net/browse/SFCC-225). |\\n| AC5 | **Any** customer navigating directly to the product PDP URL — regardless of customer group or login state — sees the PDP in full and can add to cart and purchase. |\\n| AC6 | **Any** customer navigating directly to the VIC Exclusive PLP URL sees the PLP in full, and can add to cart and purchase from it. |\\n| AC7 | Setting the flag back to false returns the product to normal listing and search behaviour for all customers on the next index. |\\n| AC8 | The product's PDP is not indexed by search engines, and the product is not included in the Google Shopping feed. |\\n\\n---\\n\\n2. It is not buildable, and the evidence is in this repository rather than requiring a sandbox spike.\\n\\n`int_globale_headless` — a production, vendor-certified cartridge in this project's cartridge path — registers the hook:\\n\\nwide760json{ \\\"name\\\": \\\"dw.ocapi.shop.product\\\\_search.beforeGET\\\", \\\"script\\\": \\\"./cartridge/scripts/hooks/productSearch.js\\\" }\\n\\nSo the hook exists, and `docs/Global-e/ocapi-scapi-gap-analysis.md` confirms this cartridge \\\"already registers the full set of **SCAPI-side hooks**\\\" — hook *availability* under SCAPI was never the problem. The problem is the signature. Its implementation is:\\n\\nwide760javascript// vendor/globale-v24.5.0/.../hooks/productSearch.js:8\\nexports.beforeGET = function () {\\n// ... sets request locale, initialises a Global-e session. Nothing else.\\n};\\n\\n**No arguments.** There is no search request, no refinement collection, no query parameters — nothing to mutate. It is a side-effect-only lifecycle hook (per the OCAPI `beforeGET` contract it can also return a `Status` to abort the request, which is of no use here). A refinement cannot be injected.\\n\\nThe supporting argument is the same vendor's internal consistency: it declares parameters wherever the platform supplies them — `product.beforeGET(productId)`, `basket.beforeGET(basketId)`, `customer.baskets.beforeGET(customerId)` — and declares none for `product_search`. This is a vendor that uses the arguments it is given.\\n\\n**Outstanding runtime confirmation.** The documentary evidence above is strong but is not a runtime proof — a vendor doing locale setup would not use a search argument even if one were passed. The definitive check is to log `arguments.length` from a `product_search.beforeGET` on sandbox (§6.1). It was not run for this revision because it needs deploy credentials that live in the gitignored `.env`. It is **not** a blocker: it can only overturn a rejection, and Option D does not depend on the answer. Run it before anyone revisits Option A.\\n\\n#### Option B — Separate catalog per customer segment (Discarded)\\n\\nDiscarded — requires maintaining duplicate catalog structures per segment, violates the no-catalog-restructuring constraint, and introduces significant ongoing merchandising overhead.\\n\\n#### Option C — Category-level hidden category (Discarded)\\n\\nDiscarded — SFCC category visibility is binary (all users or no users) and cannot be gated by customer group, so it would hide the product from VIC customers too, breaking AC4.\\n\\n#### Option D — PWA-side refinement injection (Recommended)\\n\\nThe PLP override at `overrides/app/pages/product-list/index.tsx` serves both the `/search` and `/category/:categoryId` routes, and already builds a `refine` array that it passes to `useProductSearch`. The change is to append an `isVICExclusive = false` refinement to that array, unless one of two carve-outs applies.\\n\\n**Why this satisfies AC3 where Option E does not.** The refinement travels with the request, so SFCC applies it as part of executing the search. `total`, pagination and per-refinement counts are all computed over the filtered result set — which is exactly what AC3's \\\"does not contribute to any refinement counts\\\" clause requires. No post-hoc correction is needed because nothing is corrected after the fact.\\n\\n**Two carve-outs**, mirroring the v0.2 logic but evaluated client-side:\\n\\n* The customer is a member of the VIC customer group — no refinement appended (AC4).\\n* The current category is flagged as the VIC Exclusive category — no refinement appended, regardless of customer group. This is what makes the AC6 direct-URL PLP render in full for everyone.\\n\\n**Two things this reuses rather than invents**, which is most of why it is cheap:\\n\\n| **Existing mechanism** | **Location** | **How v0.3 uses it** |\\n| --- | --- | --- |\\n| `REFINEMENT_DISALLOW_LIST` | `product-list/index.tsx:105`, currently `['c_isNew']` | Add `'c_isVICExclusive'` so the injected refinement never renders as a shopper-facing facet. Established precedent for hiding a `c_*` refinement — a one-line change. |\\n| `useCategory` result | `product-list/index.tsx:237`; `category` is already in scope and already read for page meta tags | Read `category.c_isVICExclusiveCategory` for the AC6 carve-out. **No additional fetch** — the data is already on hand. |\\n\\n**Design change from v0.2:** v0.2 specified a `vicExclusiveCategoryId` site preference holding the category ID. That suited a server-side hook but is awkward client-side, since a site preference is not readily available to the PWA and baking the ID into PWA config would require a deployment to change it. v0.3 instead uses an `isVICExclusiveCategory` boolean *category* custom attribute: the merchandiser flags the category in BM, and the PLP reads it off the category object it already fetches. Fewer moving parts, no deployment to change, and it stays merchandiser-controlled.\\n\\n**Accepted limitations.** Enforcement is client-side, and any non-PWA SCAPI consumer receives unsuppressed results. Both are acceptable under §1.3 — there is no confidentiality boundary, and the one consumer that genuinely matters (the Google Shopping feed) is handled separately and server-side via AC8. When [SFCC-628](https://zimmermannworkboard.atlassian.net/browse/SFCC-628) and [SFCC-629](https://zimmermannworkboard.atlassian.net/browse/SFCC-629) introduce real gating, enforcement should move server-side at that point, where it belongs.\\n\\n#### How the PWA learns VIC membership — resolved in v0.6\\n\\nv0.3 to v0.5 left this open with three speculative candidates. Removing hits is the same operation.\\n\\nIt is rejected because it filters after the search has executed. `total`, the page window and every per-refinement count are computed over the unfiltered result set, so a non-VIC customer would see correct-looking facet counts that include products they cannot see, and pages that return fewer items than requested. Recommended Option & Rationale\\n---------------------------------\\n\\n### 4.1 Recommended Option\\n\\n**Option D — PWA-side refinement injection**, with a VIC customer group carve-out and a VIC-category carve-out, plus the AC8 SEO and feed measures in §5. No PDP guard, and no SFCC search hook.\\n\\n### 4.2 Rationale\\n\\nOption D is the only remaining approach that satisfies AC2, AC3 and AC4 together. Options B and C were ruled out on constraints in v0.1. Option A is not buildable. Option E fails AC3. That leaves D by elimination — but it also stands on its own merits: the refinement is applied by SFCC at search time so all counts are correct, it reuses two mechanisms the PLP already has, and it adds no hook to the search pipeline that every storefront depends on.\\n\\nThe reason Option D is acceptable at all is §1.3. Under the v0.1 gated reading, a client-controllable filter would have been unacceptable. Under AC5 and AC6 the products are openly viewable and purchasable by anyone with the URL, so there is no confidentiality boundary for a client-side filter to fail to defend. This is worth stating explicitly because it is the one judgement in this document that a reviewer is most likely to challenge.\\n\\nThe product attribute and the category are both retained deliberately, because they do different jobs and neither substitutes for the other:\\n\\n| **Mechanism** | **Job** | **Why the other one cannot do it** |\\n| --- | --- | --- |\\n| `isVICExclusive` boolean *product* attribute | Drives search/PLP suppression (AC2, AC3), the `noindex` decision and the feed exclusion (AC8) | Category membership cannot express a negative filter — SFCC refinements are inclusive only (§2.1) |\\n| `isVICExclusiveCategory` boolean *category* attribute | Marks the AC6 direct-URL PLP as exempt from suppression | A product attribute says nothing about which listing surface the shopper is on |\\n| VIC Exclusive category itself | Drives the badge via the existing [SFCC-225](https://zimmermannworkboard.atlassian.net/browse/SFCC-225) hook, and is the landing surface for AC6 | A boolean attribute is not a browsable surface and carries no badge wiring |\\n\\n### 4.3 Trade-offs Accepted\\n\\n* **Client-side enforcement.** Accepted per §1.3 and §4.2. Should move server-side when SFCC-628/629 introduce real gating.\\n* **Non-PWA SCAPI consumers are unsuppressed.** Accepted — the consumer that matters commercially is the shopping feed, which AC8 handles separately and server-side.\\n* **Re-index lag.** A flag change is not reflected in search until the next index. In v0.1 the PDP guard acted as a second line of enforcement during that window; with the guard withdrawn there is no backstop. Benign under the current ACs — a visibility lag, not exposure — but it must be documented in the Merchandiser runbook.\\n* **Two merchandiser actions per product.** Setting the attribute and assigning the badge category are separate steps. Deriving one from the other via a job was considered and rejected: AC1 and AC7 describe a merchandiser setting the attribute directly, and a derivation job would overwrite that edit. Both are existing BM workflows; the runbook covers the pairing.\\n* **A merchandising construct is being co-opted as a visibility filter.** A search refinement definition exists to drive the shopper-facing filter panel (SPR-19, SPR-23, SPR-57). This design creates one purely so a refinement can be applied, then hides it from the UI. Given Options A, B, C and E are all ruled out there is no better instrument available — but this is the root cause of three downstream consequences that a reviewer should see stated rather than infer: the facet must be suppressed in *two* places (§5.3), an applied-filter chip would otherwise expose a one-click bypass (§5.4), and the definition sits on a BM screen that business users own and may reasonably edit (§7).\\n\\n---\\n\\n5. Technical Implementation\\n---------------------------\\n\\n### 5.1 High-Level Sequence\\n\\n1. Site Merchandiser sets `isVICExclusive = true` on the product in Business Manager and assigns it to the VIC Exclusive category (the latter drives the badge and the AC6 PLP).\\n2. Standard SFCC re-indexing propagates the attribute value to the search index.\\n3. A customer opens a PLP or runs a search. The PWA resolves VIC membership via Shopper Experience `resolveQualifiers` (cached per session) and fetches the category object it already needs for page meta.\\n4. The PLP builds its `refine` array. A customer navigates directly to a VIC Exclusive PDP URL. The PDP renders in full and the product can be added to cart and purchased — **no guard, no check, by design** (AC5).\\n9.
